# AI Daily Digest - 2026-04-29

## Anthropic

- **[Claude Commits to Remaining Ad-Free](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-is-a-space-to-think)** - Anthropic has formally committed that Claude will never show ads, publishing a post titled "Claude is a space to think" explaining why advertising incentives are fundamentally incompatible with a genuinely helpful AI assistant. The announcement is a direct contrast to OpenAI, which recently began testing ads for ChatGPT Free and Go users. Anthropic says users will never see sponsored links adjacent to conversations, and Claude's responses will never be influenced by third-party product placements.

- **[Claude Rate Limits API Now Available](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)** - Anthropic released the Rate Limits API, allowing administrators to programmatically query the rate limits configured for their organization and workspaces. This is a developer-facing quality-of-life improvement for teams managing multiple Claude deployments at scale. Additionally, Anthropic announced the deprecation of Claude Sonnet 4 and Opus 4 (non-.x versions) with retirement scheduled for June 15, 2026 — developers should migrate to Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.7.

- **[Claude Managed Agents Now in Public Beta](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)** - Anthropic launched Claude Managed Agents in public beta, a fully managed agent harness for running Claude as an autonomous agent with secure sandboxing, built-in tools, and server-sent event streaming. The service offers stable interfaces for sessions, harnesses, and sandboxes, emphasizing durable state and safer tool access for long-running agentic tasks. The `ant` CLI also launched alongside it, enabling faster API interaction and native Claude Code integration.

## OpenAI

- **[OpenAI Models and Codex Now on Amazon Bedrock (Limited Preview)](https://openai.com/index/openai-on-aws/)** - OpenAI and AWS launched a major partnership expansion on April 28, bringing OpenAI's frontier models (including GPT-5.5), the Codex coding agent, and a new Amazon Bedrock Managed Agents service powered by OpenAI into limited preview. This comes just one day after OpenAI and Microsoft renegotiated their exclusivity deal, freeing OpenAI to serve all its products on any cloud provider. AWS CEO Matt Garman said at a San Francisco launch event: "Their production applications run in AWS. Their data is in AWS. They trust the security of AWS, and we've forced them for the last couple of years, to get great OpenAI models, to go to other places."

- **[Microsoft-OpenAI Partnership Renegotiated, Exclusivity Ends](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/04/27/the-next-phase-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/)** - Microsoft and OpenAI announced an amended partnership on April 27 that gives OpenAI the freedom to serve all its products to customers across any cloud provider, not just Azure. Microsoft remains OpenAI's primary cloud partner — OpenAI products will ship first on Azure unless Microsoft cannot support the necessary capabilities — and Microsoft's license to OpenAI IP continues through 2032. Revenue share payments continue through 2030 but are now subject to a total cap.

- **[OpenAI Discontinues Sora Video App](https://www.ai-market-watch.com/news/openai-sets-april-26-2026-as-discontinuation-date-for-sora-web-and-app-experienc-diyse6)** - OpenAI shut down its Sora web and app experiences on April 26, 2026, with the Sora API following on September 24. The company is directing users to export content before the final cutoff, and refunds for unused credits can be applied to Codex instead — a telling signal that OpenAI views agentic coding as its higher strategic priority. The shutdown marks a rare public product cancellation from a frontier lab and signals the company's willingness to kill a high-profile but commercially underperforming generative media offering.

## Google AI

- **[Google Launches Deep Research and Deep Research Max Powered by Gemini 3.1 Pro](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/next-generation-gemini-deep-research/)** - Google DeepMind released two new autonomous research agents via the Gemini API on April 21: Deep Research (optimized for speed and lower latency) and Deep Research Max (designed for maximum comprehensiveness using extended test-time compute). Both are built on Gemini 3.1 Pro, support MCP for connecting to private data sources, and can generate native charts and infographics. Gemini 3.1 Pro scored 85.9 on OpenAI's BrowseComp benchmark — over 25 points higher than its Gemini 3 Pro predecessor.

- **[Accenture and Google Cloud Launch Gemini Enterprise Acceleration Program](https://www.googlecloudpresscorner.com/2026-04-22-Accenture-and-Google-Cloud-Expand-Partnership-to-Scale-Agentic-Transformation-for-Global-Enterprises-with-Gemini-Enterprise)** - Announced at Google Cloud Next '26 (April 22-24, Las Vegas), the Gemini Enterprise Acceleration Program combines Google Cloud engineering, DeepMind model access, and Accenture's forward-deployed engineers to help enterprises deploy specialized AI agents at scale. The program includes early access to frontier Gemini models and a new Generative Content OS for marketing organizations powered by Gemini 3.1 Flash Image and Veo. Accenture earned Google Cloud's 2026 Global Services Partner of the Year award for the fourth consecutive year.

## Developer Tools & Vibe Coding

- **[Lovable Launches Vibe-Coding App on iOS and Android](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/28/lovable-launches-its-vibe-coding-app-on-ios-and-android/)** - Lovable, the no-code AI app builder, launched its mobile app on both the Apple App Store and Google Play on April 28, becoming one of the first vibe-coding platforms to successfully navigate Apple's recent crackdown on the category. The app allows builders to submit voice or text prompts from anywhere — letting Lovable's agent run autonomously — and syncs seamlessly between phone and desktop. To comply with Apple's guidelines (which ban apps from downloading new code post-approval), Lovable shifted app previews to web browsers rather than running them inside the host app. The Android version already showed 100K+ downloads on Google Play shortly after launch.

- **[GitHub Copilot Moving to Usage-Based Billing on June 1](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)** - GitHub announced on April 27 that all Copilot plans will transition from premium request counts to token-based "GitHub AI Credits" billing on June 1, 2026, where 1 AI Credit = $0.01 USD. Base plan prices remain unchanged (Pro: $10/mo, Pro+: $39/mo, Business: $19/user/mo, Enterprise: $39/user/mo), but heavy agentic workflows — which GitHub says now regularly cost more per session than the monthly plan price — will incur variable overage charges. A preview bill experience will launch in early May so admins can see projected costs before the transition.

- **[Apple's Vibe-Coding App Crackdown Reshaping the Ecosystem](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/28/lovable-launches-its-vibe-coding-app-on-ios-and-android/)** - Apple recently blocked updates to popular vibe-coding tools including Replit and Vibecode, citing developer guideline violations around apps that download new code or change their functionality post-install. The app Anything was temporarily removed from the App Store before returning after making changes. Vibe-coding platforms are adapting by shifting generated-app previews to web browsers — Lovable's successful iOS launch this week shows the model can work within Apple's rules while still delivering core functionality.
